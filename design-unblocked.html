Scaling Design Through AI

I used AI to enable 20 PMs to prototype on their own — so my 5 designers could focus on the hard problems.

Result8 → 3.5 days design-to-dev handoff~1 month → 12 days on large products
01The problem

5 designers. 20 PMs. One queue.

Every request, big or small, waited for the same five people.

Six kinds of request
  • Quick UI fix
  • Small interaction change
  • Ops workflow experiment
  • Pilot prototype
  • New requirement
  • New flow to validate
1 queue

Design became the bottleneck

5 designers~20 PMs

Not “how can designers work faster?” but “why does every request need a designer?”

So I split the work by complexity
Tier A · Designer-ledHard problemsStrategy, complex UX, critical journeys
Tier B · Product + DesignExperimentsPrototypes, pilots, early validation
Tier C · AI-assistedRepeatable workSmall changes, visual QA
02Fixing the front door

Before touching AI, I fixed the intake.

Requirements arrived with gaps, so Design filled them in, round after round.

Before · the loop
PM idea→Designer asks questions→More context→First design→Stakeholder feedback→More clarification→↺ Repeat
After · a 6-question Design Intake
  1. What are we solving?
  2. Why does it matter?
  3. Who is affected?
  4. What needs to be designed?
  5. What needs to be tested?
  6. What does “done” look like?

Decides whether something needs Design, and which tier. Pressure-tested with Product so it would be adopted.

03Five moves

Five small fixes, one bottleneck each.

04Bringing people along

The hardest part was trust, on both sides.

Designers askedWhy train PMs to do our job?

I framed it as freeing their bandwidth for high-impact work. Designers became the experts PMs consult, not tools executing requests.

PMs saidFigma Make isn’t as good as Lovable.

I ran the same prompt in both. Results were comparable, and Figma Make’s were far easier for Design to take to production.

The 30-second version

I removed Design as the bottleneck for 20 PMs by giving them AI prototyping inside our design system — so 5 designers could focus on the hard problems.

The problem
5 designers, ~20 PMs, one queue. Small fixes and big flows all waited for a designer, while complex work stalled.
What I did
Fixed intake, split work into three tiers by complexity, and taught PMs to prototype in Figma Make using our Design System. Claude turned legacy screens into structured flows; AI took the first pass at visual QA.
The result
Across 4 roadmap projects over 4 months, design-to-dev handoff dropped from 8 to 3.5 working days on medium items and from ~1 month to 12 days on large ones. A new SOP cut PM review time from ~3 days to 1. The model is now scaling across Product pods.
