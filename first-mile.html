01My role

My first project at Delhivery, and I took it on as an IC.

As Design Manager, I led a strategic initiative to improve how First Mile Executives plan routes, carry out pickups and scan shipments. Through field immersion, stakeholder alignment, systems thinking and iterative pilots, we turned a pickup-request-centric workflow into a stop-centric operating model.

It was my first project at Delhivery and I had limited exposure to the domain, so I picked it up midway as an IC to learn First Mile operations and how the new organisation worked.

04Research

We went where the pickups happen.

  • Field shadowingDesigners shadowed FEs and coordinators on live runs, from scanning shipments under time pressure to navigating broken flows, so we built from on-ground empathy, not assumptions.
  • User interviewsSessions with FM centre managers, team leads and PMs uncovered operational gaps, team frustrations and backend complexity that affect delivery performance.
05Key problems

Riders planned routes in their heads and searched for every pickup.

Route & dispatch

  • 6%Manual route planningRoutes aren't system-driven; FEs remember seller locations and plan in their heads, so 6% of PURs stay unattempted.
  • 36%Mid-day disruptionsAbout 36% of PURs are added mid-day, forcing FEs to re-plan while already on the road.
  • 10–12 daysSlow onboardingNew FEs need 10–12 days to learn seller clusters and the best order to visit them.
  • 9%PUR transfersFinding PURs that can't be serviced and moving them to another dispatch is slow; about 9% get transferred.

Scanning

  • Every stopManual PUR selectionAt every stop, FEs must search for and open the right PUR before they can scan packages.
  • 4×Scanning time at one locationThe system doesn't group pickups from the same seller, so FEs sort packages by hand: 4× longer scanning at one location.
06Proposed solutions

Four ideas to cut the hunting and the re-planning.

  1. 01Map viewAn integrated map with stop and PUR pins and the FE's current location.
  2. 02Stop sequencingNearby sellers grouped into one STOP, where the FE parks and attempts every PUR, then stops sequenced to cut repeat travel.
  3. 03PUR managementSpot new (36%), escalated and failed-to-pickup PURs on the move, without searching.
  4. 04Global scanScan any package without picking a PUR first; the system links it and shows picked vs not-picked per PUR.
07Iterations

Design evolving with each review.

Home screen

  • Map of the FE's location relative to assigned stops
  • Status chips: New, FTP, Escalated, Expired
  • Summary bar with Dispatch ID and stop, PUR and shipment counts
  • “Nearby stops” sorted by current GPS

Less time searching, more time picking up.

Stop details

  • Map of every pickup point in the stop area
  • Clear time slots to plan the schedule
  • Address, expected shipments, navigate and call seller
  • A prominent Global Scan entry point

Less time per stop, higher pickup rate.

08Final solution

Stops first, scanning in one step.

01

Stops & sequencing

Route sequencing is done stop-wise, not PUR-wise, and ordered by distance: closest first. Riders always know where to go next.

02

PUR view

One listing of every pickup request inside a stop, with call and navigate straight to the seller in Google Maps.

03

Map view

Stops pinned on a map so FEs understand the system-planned route at a glance.

04

One-step scanning

From four steps (search seller → open PUR → tap Start → scan) to one: scan, and the package maps itself to its PUR.

09Pilot feedback

What riders told us in the pilot.

  • Stops

    The concept is understood, but it will take some getting used to. FEs realised the list would be shorter, focusing more strongly on area-level information.

  • Slots

    FEs here don't follow slots, so this won't affect them much. For a few sellers they know availability constraints and plan around them.

  • Sequencing

    Expected to be especially useful for newer FEs.

  • Global scan

    Strong enthusiasm: FEs said they'll no longer need to search for the PUR address, a big efficiency gain.
